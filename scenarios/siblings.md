Alex and Jordan are dividing the estate their late parent left behind. They
agree on most of it — the $6,000 in funeral costs comes off the top — but not on
the $8,000 their parent gave Jordan a few years ago: Alex says it was an advance
on the inheritance that should reduce Jordan's share; Jordan remembers it as a
gift, freely given.

The kernel certifies the estate math, gently surfaces the disputed figure as a
number to reconcile rather than an accusation, and reduces the whole question to
one undecided point — was that money an advance, or a gift? — handing it back to
the two of them instead of pretending to decide it. The family piano, Mum's
paintings, the good china, and Dad's woodworking tools are split fairly.
